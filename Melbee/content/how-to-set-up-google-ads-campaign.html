Before You Create a Campaign: The 3 Things That Have to Be in Place

Most Google Ads failures happen before the first ad is ever written. The campaign structure is set up, money starts flowing, and results are disappointing — not because the ads are bad, but because the foundation wasn’t ready. Before you create a single campaign, make sure three things are in place:

  1. Conversion tracking is installed. If you can’t measure which keywords generate calls or form submissions, you cannot optimize the campaign. Install call tracking and form submission tracking before spending a dollar.
  2. Your landing page is ready. A Google Ad sends traffic to a page. If that page is slow, unclear, or doesn’t have a prominent call to action, you’ll get clicks with no conversions. Test your landing page before launching.
  3. Your budget is realistic. In competitive local service markets, clicks cost $3–$15 each. A $5/day budget generates 1–2 clicks per day — not enough data to optimize. Plan for at least $30–$50/day ($900–$1,500/month) to generate meaningful results in most service markets.

Step 1: Campaign Type and Goal

In Google Ads, select Search campaign with the goal of Leads. This focuses Google’s system on showing your ads to people actively searching, and optimizes toward conversion actions (calls and form fills) rather than clicks.

Step 2: Geographic Targeting

Set your location to your actual service area — the specific cities, counties, or radius around your business where you serve clients. Do not target the entire US, or even an entire state, unless you genuinely serve that area. Narrower targeting = higher relevance = lower cost per lead.

Important: set “Presence” targeting, not “Presence or interest.” This ensures your ad shows to people physically located in your target area, not just people who have shown interest in it.

Step 3: Keyword Strategy

Start with 15–25 highly relevant keywords, organized into tightly themed ad groups. Each ad group should have 3–5 keywords with the same intent. Example structure:

  • Ad Group 1 — Core service: “digital marketing agency,” “marketing agency near me,” “hire marketing agency”
  • Ad Group 2 — Specific service: “Google Ads management,” “run Google Ads for me,” “Google Ads agency”
  • Ad Group 3 — Local: “marketing agency Birmingham AL,” “marketing agency Alabama”

Use phrase match for most keywords to start. Add a negative keyword list from day one: terms like “free,” “jobs,” “course,” “DIY,” “template” — anything indicating someone who won’t become a client.

Step 4: Ad Copy

Write Responsive Search Ads with 10–15 headlines and 4 descriptions. Google will mix and match to find the best-performing combinations. Key principles:

  • Include your primary keyword in at least 2 headlines
  • State a clear differentiator (in-house team, 15 years experience, 500+ clients)
  • Include social proof if you have it (Google reviews, specific results)
  • Every description should end with a specific call to action
  • Don’t use the same message in every headline — variety lets Google test more combinations

Step 5: Bidding Strategy

Start with Maximize Clicks with a maximum CPC bid cap. This is the safest starting strategy for a new campaign because it gets traffic without overpaying per click. After 30–60 days and 30+ conversions, switch to Target CPA (Target Cost Per Acquisition) to optimize toward leads instead of just clicks.

Do not use Smart Bidding strategies (Target CPA, Target ROAS) from day one — they require conversion data to work. Without data, smart bidding will spend inefficiently.

Step 6: Extensions

Add all available extensions — they increase your ad size and CTR at no extra cost:

  • Call extension with your phone number and business hours
  • Sitelink extensions with 4 links to key pages (Services, Reviews, About, Contact)
  • Callout extensions highlighting your differentiators (Free Estimates, 15+ Years Experience, Licensed & Insured)
  • Location extension linking to your Google Business Profile

Week 1 After Launch: What to Watch

In the first week, check the Search Terms report daily. This shows the actual queries triggering your ads. Add anything irrelevant to your negative keyword list immediately. This single habit will save 20–30% of your budget from wasted spend in the first 30 days.

Don’t change bids or pause keywords in week 1 — you need data before you can optimize. Let it run for at least 2 weeks before making meaningful changes.
