The Wrong Question — And the Right One

Business owners frequently ask “Should I run Meta Ads or Google Ads?” It’s the wrong question. The right question is: “What role do I need each platform to play in my marketing strategy, and how much should I invest in each right now?”

Both platforms work. Both can generate significant ROI for service businesses. But they work differently, for different psychological reasons, at different stages of the buyer journey.

The Core Difference: Intent vs. Interest

Google Ads captures intent. Someone types “electrician Birmingham AL” into Google — they have a specific problem and they’re actively looking for a solution. Your ad shows up at the moment of maximum buying intent. The lead quality is high because the person already decided they need what you offer.

Meta Ads create interest. Someone is scrolling their Instagram feed when your video of a stunning kitchen renovation appears. They weren’t looking for countertops — but now they’re thinking about it. Meta reaches people before they’ve searched. It plants the idea, builds desire, and captures leads from a much larger potential audience than those who are actively searching.

When Google Ads Wins

Google Ads outperforms Meta when:

  • Your service solves an urgent problem (emergency plumbing, HVAC repair, legal issues)
  • Your buyers search for exactly what you offer using specific search terms
  • You need leads within the next 30–60 days
  • Your product or service is difficult to explain visually

When Meta Ads Win

Meta outperforms Google when:

  • Your work is visual (home renovation, design, photography, food)
  • Your buyer needs to be inspired before they’ll search
  • You want to build brand awareness in a specific demographic and geography
  • Your search volume is low (few people are searching, but many would want your service if they saw it)
  • You want to retarget website visitors and past customers

Cost Comparison

Google Ads cost per click is typically higher than Meta — often $3–$15 per click for service keywords vs. $0.50–$3.00 for Meta. But this doesn’t mean Google is more expensive per lead. Google’s higher-intent traffic often converts at a higher rate, bringing the cost per lead to a comparable level. What matters is cost per lead and cost per client, not cost per click.

The Combined Strategy: How They Work Together

The businesses generating the most predictable revenue from digital advertising are using both platforms in complementary roles:

  • Meta Ads: Create awareness, build desire, generate initial leads from people who weren’t searching
  • Google Ads: Capture people who ARE searching — including people who saw your Meta Ads and are now researching you
  • Retargeting (Meta): Re-engage everyone who visited your website from any source

When someone sees your Meta video, then later searches Google and sees your ad, and then visits your site and gets retargeted — the conversion rate for that person is dramatically higher than any single touchpoint alone.

Budget Allocation: A Starting Framework

For a $1,500/month advertising budget, we recommend:

  • $1,000–$1,100 to Google Ads (capture active searchers)
  • $400–$500 to Meta Ads (awareness + lead generation + retargeting)

As budget increases, scale both proportionally — but maintain Google as the primary lead driver until you have the data to optimize your Meta campaigns for equivalent cost per lead.

Which Should You Start With?

If you’re starting from zero: Google Ads first, for 60–90 days, to generate leads and revenue to reinvest. Then add Meta Ads to expand your reach. If your business is visual and you already have good video content: start both simultaneously.
