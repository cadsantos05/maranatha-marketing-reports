The Channel With the Best ROI That Most Businesses Ignore

Email marketing consistently delivers $36–$42 in return for every $1 spent — the highest ROI of any digital marketing channel. Yet most service businesses either don’t have an email list at all, or they have one and never use it consistently. This represents one of the most significant untapped revenue opportunities in small business marketing.

What an Email List Actually Gives You

Social media platforms can change their algorithms, reduce your organic reach to near zero, or suspend your account. Google can update its ranking factors and drop your traffic overnight. Your email list is owned by you. The 500 or 5,000 people on your list are contacts you can reach directly, at any time, regardless of what any platform decides to do.

In a digital landscape where platform dependency creates real business risk, an email list is one of the most valuable assets a service business can build.

Building a List Worth Having

An email list is only valuable if the people on it actually want to hear from you. The fastest way to build a quality list:

  • Existing clients: Every client who has worked with you should be on your list. They already trust you. They’re your highest-probability source of repeat business and referrals.
  • Leads who didn’t convert: Add every inbound lead to a nurture list. A prospect who wasn’t ready to buy in March might be ready in July. Staying in their inbox keeps you in mind.
  • Website opt-in with a value exchange: A free guide, checklist, or assessment that’s genuinely useful to your target client. “Download: 10 Questions to Ask Before Hiring a Marketing Agency” will convert visitors into subscribers who have clearly signaled interest in your service.
  • Meta Ads lead list: Every lead from your Meta Ads Instant Forms should be added to your email list for long-term nurturing, even if they don’t convert immediately.

What to Send and How Often

The most common email marketing question: what do I write? The answer is simpler than most people think — write about what your clients care about, not what you want to sell.

For a service business, a sustainable email rhythm looks like:

  • Monthly newsletter: One insight, one recent project or result, one useful tip, one soft CTA (“If you’re thinking about X, we’d love to help.”)
  • Quarterly educational email: A longer piece of content — a guide, a case study, a detailed explanation of something relevant to your clients
  • Promotional email (2–3x per year): A direct offer — a seasonal promotion, new service announcement, or limited availability message

Consistency matters more than frequency. One email per month, sent reliably for two years, builds more trust than 10 emails in January and nothing until June.

The Automated Sequences That Do the Heavy Lifting

Beyond broadcast emails (sent to your whole list), automated sequences run in the background and nurture leads based on their behavior:

  • Welcome sequence (3–5 emails over 2 weeks): When someone joins your list, introduce your business, share your best content, and make a soft offer. This is your best window to convert a new subscriber.
  • Lead nurture sequence: For prospects who inquired but didn’t hire. 6–10 emails over 60 days sharing case studies, answering common objections, and reminding them you’re available.
  • Re-engagement sequence: For clients who haven’t heard from you in 90+ days. “It’s been a while — here’s what we’ve been working on, and here’s how we might be able to help you again.”

Measuring Email Performance

Three metrics matter: open rate (aim for 25–35% for service businesses), click-through rate (aim for 2–5%), and unsubscribe rate (keep under 0.3% per send). If open rates drop below 20%, your subject lines need work or your list quality has declined. If unsubscribe rates spike, your sending frequency or content relevance needs adjusting.
