Likes Are Not Leads

A business owner runs a Facebook ad. The post gets 200 likes and 40 shares. Everyone tells them it’s going well. But the phone isn’t ringing. The problem isn’t the engagement — it’s the campaign objective. When your ad is optimized for engagement, Meta shows it to people most likely to like and share. When it’s optimized for leads, Meta shows it to people most likely to fill out a form.

The objective you choose in Meta Ads Manager determines who sees your ad. Choose the wrong objective and you can run a technically perfect ad to the wrong audience — and generate zero revenue from it.

Step 1: Set the Right Objective

For lead generation, use the Leads objective. This tells Meta’s algorithm to find people in your target audience who are most likely to fill out a form. Within the Leads objective, choose Instant Forms — it keeps the user in the app, reducing friction and increasing completion rate.

Avoid using the Engagement or Awareness objectives for lead generation campaigns. They optimize for the wrong behavior.

Step 2: Build the Right Audience

The most common Meta Ads mistake is targeting too broadly or using irrelevant interest categories. For a service business in Alabama, the right audience is:

  • Geographic: your specific service cities and a realistic radius
  • Age: 28–65 for most B2C services (adjust based on your actual client profile)
  • Income: if Meta offers income-based targeting in your area, use top 25–50%
  • Interests: homeownership, home improvement, your specific category (marketing, business, finance, etc.)
  • Behavior: homeowners, frequent travelers, small business owners (depending on your target)

Or use Meta’s Advantage+ Audience — enter a detailed description of your ideal client and let the algorithm find them. For most businesses, this outperforms manual audience building after a 2-week learning period.

Step 3: Create a Lead-Focused Ad

Lead-focused ads don’t look like promotional posters. They look like content. The creative — whether video or image — should open with a problem your target client recognizes (“Still waiting on your countertop?” / “Marketing budget going nowhere?”) and quickly provide a reason to act now.

For service businesses, video almost always outperforms static images for cold audiences. Show your work. Show your process. Show a client result. People buy outcomes, not descriptions.

Step 4: Build Your Instant Form for Quality Leads

Your Instant Form is where most lead quality is won or lost. Use the Higher Intent form type — it adds a review screen before submission, which reduces casual form fills and improves lead quality.

Ask 3–4 questions: name, phone, what service they’re interested in, and one qualifying question specific to your business (“What’s your timeline?”, “What’s your approximate budget range?”). This pre-qualifies the lead before you’ve spoken to them.

Step 5: Follow Up Within 2 Hours

This is where most Meta lead generation falls apart. The ad works, the form is filled out, and then the lead sits unanswered for 12–24 hours. Meta leads have a very short attention span — they submitted a form while scrolling, which means they were interested but not necessarily urgent. A fast response is what converts that momentary interest into an actual conversation.

Build a system: when a lead comes in, an automatic text goes out within 5 minutes, and a human calls within 2 hours. This single process improvement will double your Meta Ads conversion rate.

What to Expect in the First 30 Days

In the first 7 days, don’t touch the campaign. Meta needs this learning period to optimize. After day 7, check your cost per lead. If it’s within your acceptable range, let it run. If it’s too high, check your creative (is it compelling?), your form completion rate (are people dropping off?), and your audience (is it specific enough?).

By day 30, you should have enough data to identify which ad variation, which audience segment, and which form is performing best. Scale the winner. Pause the underperformers.
