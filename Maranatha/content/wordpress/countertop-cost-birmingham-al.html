Countertop pricing is one of the most common questions homeowners in Birmingham ask when planning a kitchen or bathroom remodel. The honest answer is that it varies — significantly — based on the material you choose, the complexity of the installation, and the specific slab. However, you can build a reasonable budget if you understand how pricing works and what drives costs up or down.

This guide breaks down countertop costs in the Birmingham, Alabama market by material, explains what is included in a typical quote, and outlines the factors that affect your final price.

How Countertop Pricing Works

Most countertop fabricators in Birmingham price by the square foot. That per-square-foot figure includes the cost of the raw slab material and the fabrication — cutting, edging, and polishing to fit your countertop dimensions. Installation is often quoted separately or bundled into the total depending on the company.

To estimate your total, you need your countertop square footage. Measure the length and depth of each countertop section, calculate the square footage for each, and add them together. Additionally, do not forget islands, bathroom vanities, or any other surfaces you are replacing. Most residential kitchen countertops fall in the range of 30 to 60 square feet, with larger kitchens or those with substantial islands running higher.

Countertop Costs by Material in Birmingham

These ranges reflect the Birmingham, Alabama market. They include material and fabrication but may or may not include installation depending on the company — so always confirm what is included when you get a quote.

Granite

Granite pricing in Birmingham typically runs from $50 to $130 per square foot installed, depending on the slab. Standard granite slabs in common colors — beige, black, gray, brown — sit at the lower end of that range. More unusual colors, exotic patterns, and slabs imported from specific quarries in Brazil, India, or Italy tend to cost more.

Overall, granite remains one of the most cost-competitive natural stone options available in the Birmingham market. The range of available price points makes it accessible for most budgets.

Quartz

Quartz countertops in Birmingham typically range from $60 to $140 per square foot installed. Entry-level quartz from major manufacturers sits at the lower end. Premium lines from brands like Cambria, which is made in the USA, or high-design collections from Silestone and Caesarstone, push toward the upper end.

Quartz pricing is generally more predictable than natural stone because it is a manufactured product with consistent pricing from manufacturers. That said, the wide range of available products means costs can still vary considerably depending on what you choose.

Quartzite

Quartzite is a natural stone with a narrower supply than granite, which tends to push prices higher. In Birmingham, quartzite typically ranges from $80 to $160 per square foot installed, with premium and exotic varieties — Super White, Taj Mahal, Sea Pearl — at the upper end.

Indeed, the high demand for quartzite varieties that resemble marble, combined with limited availability, keeps prices elevated compared to most granite options.

Marble

Marble in Birmingham typically runs from $75 to $200 per square foot installed, with significant variation based on the specific type. Carrara marble, the most common variety used in American homes, sits at the lower end. Calacatta and other premium Italian marbles are considerably more expensive. Moreover, the maintenance demands of marble are part of the cost calculation beyond just the initial price.

Porcelain Slab

Large-format porcelain countertops are a newer option in the Birmingham market. Pricing typically falls in the range of $70 to $140 per square foot installed. Porcelain requires careful handling during fabrication, and not all fabricators work with it — which can limit your options locally. Therefore, verify that your fabricator has specific porcelain experience.

What Affects the Total Price

Within any material category, several factors can move the final price up or down significantly.

Slab Selection

Within a material like granite or quartzite, there is a wide range of slabs at different price points. Standard, commonly available slabs cost less. Rare colors, dramatic veining patterns, or imported slabs from specific quarries command a premium. When you visit a showroom, slabs are often grouped by tier — the tier you choose has a direct impact on your total.

Countertop Square Footage

Larger countertops cost more in total, though the per-square-foot price does not necessarily change. Moreover, more square footage means there is more waste material from cutting, which affects how many slabs need to be purchased.

Edge Profiles

The edge detail on your countertop — the shape of the finished edge — affects fabrication cost. Simple profiles like an eased edge or straight edge are standard and typically included in a base quote. In contrast, more decorative profiles — ogee, dupont, waterfall, beveled — add cost because they require more fabrication time and specialized equipment.

Cutouts

Each cutout in a countertop — for a sink, cooktop, or outlet pop-up — adds fabrication cost. The type of cutout matters: an undermount sink cutout is more complex than a drop-in cutout. Cooktop cutouts and other custom openings add additional cost.

Number of Seams

Kitchens with L-shaped, U-shaped, or particularly long countertops require seams where slabs meet. Seam placement requires planning and skilled fabrication. As a result, more seams generally add cost.

Demolition and Removal

If your project involves removing existing countertops before installation, that work may or may not be included in your quote. Ask specifically. Some companies include removal and disposal; others charge separately.

Getting an Accurate Quote

The only way to get an accurate price for your specific project is to request quotes from fabricators who can visit your kitchen, take measurements, and review the slabs you are considering.

When comparing quotes, make sure each one covers the same scope of work. A quote that includes material, fabrication, installation, and removal of old countertops is not directly comparable to one that only covers material and fabrication. Therefore, ask each company to break down what is and is not included.

Also ask about the specific slab — not just the material. Two quotes for "granite" can differ by $50 per square foot depending on which granite slabs are being compared.

Is the Cheapest Quote the Best Deal?

Not always. Countertop fabrication quality varies, and the cost of a poorly executed installation shows up over time — in misaligned seams, uneven edges, or slabs that were not properly supported during installation.

Instead, look for a fabricator who can show you completed projects, has experienced staff handling the fabrication, and is transparent about what you are getting for the price. A slightly higher quote from a company with a strong track record is often a better investment than the lowest bid.
